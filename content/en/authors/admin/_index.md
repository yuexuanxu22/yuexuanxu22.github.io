I am a **computational and genetic epidemiologist** and **Postdoctoral Research Scientist** at **Columbia University Irving Medical Center**, mentored by [Dr. Richard Mayeux](https://www.neurology.columbia.edu/profile/richard-p-mayeux-md). I received my **PhD with a focus on genetic epidemiology** from the University of Wisconsin–Madison, where I trained with [Dr. Corinne Engelman](https://pophealth.wisc.edu/staff/engelman-corinne/), and a **Master of Public Policy** from the University of Southern California.

My research focuses on the **genetic and molecular epidemiology of Alzheimer’s disease and related dementias**, with an emphasis on **disease heterogeneity, early risk prediction, and diverse populations**. I integrate large-scale, multi-cohort genetic and genomic data with imaging, plasma, and CSF biomarkers, including p-tau217, and multi-omic data such as metabolomics, proteomics, transcriptomics, and epigenomics. I apply statistical, longitudinal, and machine-learning approaches to investigate genetic and molecular risk factors, gene–environment interactions, cognitive and disease trajectories, and biologically informed risk prediction and disease subtyping.

My first-author work has been published in leading journals including **The Lancet Neurology, Alzheimer’s & Dementia**, and **The Lancet Regional Health – Americas**. My research has involved large prospective cohorts and diverse study populations and has received national and international press coverage. My broader goal is to improve the prevention, early detection, and individualized prediction of Alzheimer’s disease and related dementias.
